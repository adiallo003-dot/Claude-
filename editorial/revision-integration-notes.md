## 2. Compression pass — what was (and was not) cut

Honest finding: **the manuscript was already tightly edited, so this pass was deliberately light.** The largest compression had already been done by the author on Part XI (the PDF, "compressed roughly a third"). Elsewhere, most apparent repetition is your **deliberate device** — a chapter-opening epigraph echoed in a closing line, and a few signature refrains ("The mines are not the test. The state is the test."; "The country generates brokers faster than it generates producers."; "A successful state uses foreign expertise as a bridge / substitute"). These were **preserved**, because removing them would flatten the book's voice, not tighten its argument.

**Cut (no argument lost):**

- **Part XIII (What Must Never Happen):** removed the standalone closing line *"An asset that cannot be maintained is not an investment. It is an expenditure."* — it duplicated, verbatim, a sentence four lines above (the maintenance prohibition) rather than echoing the chapter's epigraph, so it read as accidental repetition at close range. The sentence still appears once, in its bullet.

**Preserved deliberately (flagged, not cut):**

- **Front-matter overlap.** *The Doctrine in One Page* and *The Shape of the Argument* both open with a near-identical "The claim…" statement and both close on the mines/state test. This is genuine redundancy, but each page serves a distinct reader (a scannable summary card vs. the three-movement logical spine), and consolidating them is an **authorial decision, not mechanical de-duplication.** Left intact. If you want them merged, say so and I'll do it.
- **The "throttle / saving paces the inflow" paragraph (Part VI)** restates the Absorption Principle several ways before reaching its genuinely new point. Tightening it would mean rewriting your sentences, which risks your voice; I did not do that without your sign-off. Flag it if you'd like a proposed tightening to review.

I did not manufacture cuts to appear productive. The compression is modest because the text earned it.
