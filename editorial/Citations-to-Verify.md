# Citations & Verification Checklist — *Beyond Simandou*

Every factual, statistical, and named-example claim in the manuscript, keyed to
the chapter where it appears. The book's argument does not change based on these;
but for a published work — especially one intended to influence policy — each
figure should be traceable to a credible, dated source before release.

**Recommendation:** confirm each figure below, then add either a short *"A Note
on Sources"* page or numbered endnotes. Where a figure cannot be confirmed,
consider softening to a sourced range or attributing it ("according to…").

Legend: ◆ = quantitative claim · ▣ = named example/comparison · ⬡ =
characterization to verify.

---

## Front matter — The Architecture / The Doctrine in One Page
- ◆ 300,000 young Guineans enter the labor market each year.
- ◆ 15.5 million hectares of arable land; only 25% cultivated.

## Part One — The Misallocation
- ◆ 300,000 young Guineans enter the labor market annually *(recurs)*.
- ◆ Median age in Guinea is 18.
- ◆ "More than half the population has never known an economy that rewarded production over access" *(follows from median age — verify framing)*.
- ◆ 34% of young Guineans are NEET (not in employment, education, or training).
- ◆ 88% of young Guineans disapprove of the government's performance on job creation. *(Identify the survey, year, and sample — e.g., Afrobarometer or similar.)*
- ⬡ Characterization of the Mediterranean crossing and the "Nicaragua route to the United States" as taken by "the most resourceful, not the most desperate." *(Supportable as analysis, but flag as interpretive rather than statistical.)*

## Part Two — The Three Economies
- ◆ Women provide more than 60% of Guinea's agricultural labor.
- ◆ Women own less than 10% of agricultural land.
- ◆ Women own 40% of registered small businesses.
- ⬡ "Guinean law does not forbid women from owning land" — confirm against current statute (land tenure code / civil code).

## Part Three — Simandou Is a Stress Test, Not a Strategy
- ▣ "Nigeria earned hundreds of billions from petroleum" while most citizens remained poor.
- ▣ Venezuela — oil revenues, import dependency, state collapse when prices fell.
- ▣ Democratic Republic of Congo — extraordinary mineral wealth, population among poorest on earth.
- ▣ Botswana — diamond revenues used to build institutions/infrastructure.
- ▣ Norway — oil revenues → sovereign fund and diversified economy.
- ▣ South Korea and Singapore — transformation with few natural advantages.
  *(These comparisons are widely accepted in development literature; a single citation each, or one survey citation, will suffice.)*

## Part Four — The Sequence
- ◆ 15.5 million hectares arable; 25% cultivated *(recurs)*.
- ◆ Less than 20,000 hectares irrigated.
- ◆ 20%–30% of total agricultural production lost after harvest.

## Part Five — The Resource Constitution
- ⬡ Customs reforms "can recover billions without raising a single tax rate." *(Quantify or attribute the recoverable-revenue estimate, or phrase as illustrative.)*

## Part Six — The State Capacity Trap
- ◆ "More than 60% of agricultural machinery provided by the government or international donors sits inactive." *(Identify source/evaluation and year.)*
- ⬡ Reference to agricultural credit lines / guarantee funds that "disbursed large sums" without adequate governance. *(Confirm program(s) referenced, or keep general.)*

## Part Eight — The Forest Region Question
- ▣ Customary land claims across Kpelle, Loma, Guerze (Guerzé), and Kissi communities.
- ⬡ "Refugee flows from Sierra Leone and Liberia" and "decades of displacement." *(Well documented historically; a single source recommended.)*

## Part Nine — The Foreign Capital Doctrine
- ⬡ "Guinea's bauxite sector involves substantial Chinese financing."
- ⬡ "The Simandou development involves major Chinese-linked consortia."
  *(Both are current and verifiable; cite a recent, dated source since the
  contractual landscape evolves.)*

## Part Ten — The Guardianship Trap
- ▣ "Versions of it have shaped Egypt, Pakistan, Thailand, and Myanmar." *(Illustrative comparison; one citation or none, author's preference.)*

## Part Thirteen — The Four Corridors
- ⬡ "Mali has no port. Burkina Faso has no port. Niger has no port." *(True — all three are landlocked; no citation strictly needed, but confirm phrasing.)*
- ▣ Mandiana's "dual border position with Mali and Côte d'Ivoire." *(Geographic — verify.)*
- ⬡ "Guinea currently exports raw gold" (no domestic refining). *(Confirm current status.)*

## Conclusion — The Final Doctrine
- ◆ 130,000 registered small businesses in Guinea.
- ◆ 40% owned by women *(consistency check vs. Part Two's "40% of registered small businesses")*.
- ◆ 57% of young Guineans prefer self-employment over a government job.
- ◆ 15.5 million hectares arable *(recurs)*.
- ◆ "60% of its agricultural labor force owns less than 10% of its agricultural land" *(recurs — keep consistent with Part Two wording)*.
