# Doctrine-Only Edition — Record of Changes

**Source:** `manuscript/Beyond-Simandou.md` (full edition — unchanged, kept intact)
**Result:** `manuscript/Beyond-Simandou-Doctrine-Edition.md`

**Principle applied:** keep what explains *why* Guinea fails (diagnosis, principle, standard, test); remove what tells a government *what to build, pass, fund, or how to structure it* (the plan). Nothing was added except a small number of connective phrases to close the seams left by removal. **Every added/changed phrase is listed below.** No statistic was altered. No new argument was introduced.

## Seam edits — every added or changed phrase

These are the *only* places where words were added or altered (beyond pure deletion). All use the author's own vocabulary; none introduces a new claim.

1. **Architecture §III** — "…define how **institutions must be designed** and protected against capture" → "…define how **power must be constrained** and protected against capture"; dropped the named "Resource Constitution / Forest Region mechanism" from the list.
2. **Architecture §IV** — retitled "The Geographic *Strategy*" → "The Geographic *Principle*"; replaced the corridor sentence with the polycentric principle, stated in the author's Part XI words ("No country develops when one city monopolizes opportunity… a networked republic in which every region has a productive role… not… a political constituency connected to the capital by patronage").
3. **The Doctrine in One Page** — replaced the four-corridor bullets with one line: "**The geography.** Development must be distributed, not concentrated — a networked republic in which every region has a productive role, not a patronage relationship with the capital."
4. **Preface enumeration** — "…three National Rules, a Resource Constitution, ~~four geographic corridors~~, ~~six~~ **four** governing questions, and one test."
5. **Architecture note** — "~~six~~ **four** governing questions that only governing can resolve."
6. **Part IV** — after the kept "Why Sequence Matters," added one connective sentence to carry the surviving principle: *"The principle is not which project comes first but that capability must precede capital: money spent before the enabling layer exists produces debt, not assets."* (rephrases the author's own "money spent before the enabling layer exists produces… debt rather than… assets" + "Capability must precede capital"). Kept verbatim: the governance/sovereignty aphorisms ("…has not failed at farming. It has failed at governance." / "A country that imports food while sitting on fertile land has not yet understood sovereignty."). Changed "75 percent" → "most" in that one sentence only, because the supporting 15.5M-hectare / 25%-cultivated figures were in the removed passage; the identical figures still appear in Part I and the Architecture page.
7. **Part V** — added one connective clause to host the surviving principles: *"What such a constitution exists to protect is not a set of procedures but a set of principles."* Then verbatim author sentences (opacity = infrastructure of theft; customs = the hidden mine; military must not be a commercial actor). Minor: "currently leaking" → "that leaks" for standalone grammar.
8. **Part VIII** — replaced the cut "combined dispute resolution body" bullet with a principle clause hosting the author's verbatim reasoning: *"And legitimate adjudication cannot rest on any single authority, because the state alone lacks legitimacy, customary systems alone lack enforceability, and investors alone generate no trust."* Changed "until these three institutions are operational" → "until that governance exists."
9. **Part XII** — the rail/port prohibition kept as principle; generalized "Major **rail and port** infrastructure… **A railway or port** engineered to move only one company's **ore**…" → "Major infrastructure… Infrastructure engineered to serve only one company…"; deleted the final sentence "Such infrastructure must be common-carrier by law from the outset" (legislative instruction).
10. **Part XIII** — retitled "The Four Corridors of the Productive Republic" → "**From Projects to Systems**" (and in the Contents). Kept the systems-thinking principle; generalized the examples: "what should the **agriculture ministry** do?" → "what **a single ministry** should do"; "between **Boké and Bamako… Labé and Dakar… Siguiri and the Mali border**" → "between **one region and another**"; "power, **road and rail**, skills…" → "power, **transport**, skills…"; deleted the trailing rail-engineering sentence; "Together **the four corridors transform**… **four** outward connections, **four** productive specializations, **four** sources…" → "**The aim is to transform**… **many** outward connections, **many** productive specializations, **many** sources…"; "If yes, **the corridor** advances the doctrine" → "If yes, **it** advances the doctrine."
11. **The Final Doctrine (conclusion)** — in the "When…" litany, removed place/project tags so the images stay generic: "a truck driver ~~on the Boké–Labé corridor~~"; "a ~~Malian~~ trader chooses Guinea ~~'s port~~"; "a gold miner ~~in Mandiana~~"; "a woman ~~in Labé~~ owns the land… to build a ~~agro-processing~~ business"; "a ~~rice~~ farmer can move her harvest **to market** ~~near Nzérékoré… on the Simandou rail~~ rather than watch it rot on an unpaved road." Also: "**The four corridors** will provide connectivity" → "**Infrastructure** will provide connectivity"; deleted "The choice to make Labé a junction city rather than an afterthought is transformation."; Final Governing Question opener "…established the principle, ~~designed the institutions, mapped the geography,~~ and located the governing questions…"

Everything not listed here is the author's original text, unchanged.
